What's Shifting

In the past few months, the major tax and audit platforms have rolled out reasoning-capable AI agents that can read a trust deed, draft a Section 85 rollover memo, or stress-test a valuation model in minutes rather than days. CPA Canada's spring guidance on generative AI in assurance work, combined with the CRA's expanded digital filing requirements, has pushed mid-sized firms to start routing their "we're stuck on this one" files through AI tools before calling a specialist. The bottleneck in Canadian professional services is quietly shifting from technical knowledge to judgement and accountability.

What It Means

For a firm built on being the specialist that other accountants call when files get complicated, this cuts both ways. The routine-complex work — the reorganisations, estate freezes, and valuation second opinions that other firms used to outsource to Cedar — will increasingly get a first pass from AI inside the referring firm. But the genuinely hard files, the ones requiring integrated tax, legal, and valuation judgement under one roof, are about to get more frequent and more lucrative, because AI surfaces complexity faster than it resolves it.

The Smart Move

The sharpest boutique advisory firms are repositioning as the place referring accountants send AI-generated work for review, sign-off, and defence — essentially selling judgement and professional liability rather than hours. They are also building internal AI workflows so their own senior people spend time on the irreducible parts of a file, not on first drafts. A few are quietly productising recurring engagement types into fixed-fee reviews to capture the volume increase before competitors do.

Three Signals to Watch
1
CPA Canada's spring AI guidance The profession's standard-setters have moved from "exploring AI" to issuing practical guidance on documentation, review, and reliance — a signal that AI-assisted files are now expected, not experimental.
2
Reasoning agents inside tax software CCH, Wolters Kluwer, and Thomson Reuters have all shipped AI agents this year that draft technical memos and flag planning opportunities directly inside the workflow most Canadian mid-market firms already use.
3
Referral patterns are quietly changing Conversations at recent CPA Ontario and STEP events suggest mid-sized firms are holding files internally longer, only escalating once AI has produced a draft they cannot confidently defend — changing both the volume and the shape of specialist work.
The firms that own complex work were always selling judgement. The next eighteen months just make that more obvious — and more valuable.
