What If AI Renders Become the New First Conversation?
What's Shifting

In the past few months, generative AI tools built specifically for interior design have moved from novelty to client expectation. Wealthy clients are arriving at first meetings with photorealistic mood boards they generated themselves, and they want to talk about refinement, materiality, and wellness intent — not blank-slate concepts. The opening act of a design engagement has fundamentally changed.

What It Means

For a studio like Core Essence, this is both a compression and an elevation. The early visualisation work that used to anchor proposals is being commoditised, but the strategic layer — wellness sequencing, material sourcing, biophilic logic, the felt experience of a space — is exactly what AI cannot fake. The pressure is to stop selling renders and start selling the thinking that makes a home actually heal its occupants.

The Smart Move

The sharper studios are restructuring their first paid engagement as a Wellness Strategy intensive — a deliverable that diagnoses how a client lives, sleeps, breathes, and recovers, then translates that into spatial principles before any visual work begins. They are also adopting AI tools internally to compress concept iterations, freeing senior time for sourcing, artisan relationships, and on-site sensory decisions that no model can replicate.

Three Signals to Watch
1
Clients Arriving With Pre-Made Visuals Tools designed for residential interior generation have gone mainstream this year, and high-net-worth clients are using them before booking consultations. The first meeting is now a critique session, not a discovery one.
2
Wellness Moves From Feature to Frame Recent industry coverage shows wellness design shifting from a finishing-touch category — circadian lighting, air quality, sound — into the organising principle of the entire brief. Buyers want measurable effects on sleep and stress, not just aesthetic calm.
3
The Rise Of The Sourcing Premium As visuals become cheap, provenance becomes precious. Clients are paying noticeably more for designers with verified artisan networks, sustainable material chains, and the ability to procure pieces that algorithms cannot surface.
