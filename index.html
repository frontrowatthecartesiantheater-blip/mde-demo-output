The Back Office Just Became the Front Line
What's Shifting

Business systems work — the quoting, scheduling, invoicing, supplier coordination and admin glue that holds operators together — is being rewritten in real time by a new wave of agentic AI tools that can read emails, update records and trigger actions without a human in the middle. In recent months the major platforms have shipped agents that sit directly inside the tools small and mid-sized firms already pay for, which means the value of bolt-on automation is collapsing while the value of a properly designed system is rising fast. Buyers are starting to ask a sharper question: not "can you automate this task" but "can you run this function end to end".

What It Means

For a firm like BottBott, the work is shifting from selling software and process fixes to designing the operating model that AI agents plug into. The pressure is that generic agents will eat the easy wins — simple data entry, basic reporting, standard reconciliations — but the opportunity is much larger, because most operators have no idea how to safely hand a function over to an agent and need someone who can architect, govern and own the result.

The Smart Move

The sharpest operators in this space are repositioning from implementers to systems architects, packaging fixed-scope engagements that map a client's operations, identify where agents can run unsupervised, and install the controls that keep them honest. They are charging for outcomes — a finance function that closes itself, a sales desk that quotes overnight — rather than hours of configuration. And they are building a small library of pre-validated agent patterns so each new client engagement compounds rather than starts from scratch.

Three Signals to Watch
1
Agents Arrive Inside The Stack Microsoft, Google and the major ERP vendors have all released agent frameworks in recent months that operate inside the productivity and finance tools clients already own. The integration layer that consultants used to charge for is being absorbed into the platforms themselves.
2
Buyers Ask For Functions, Not Features RFPs and discovery calls are increasingly framed around whole functions — accounts payable, customer onboarding, field scheduling — rather than discrete software needs. Decision-makers want a working capability handed over, not a toolkit to assemble.
3
Governance Becomes The Bottleneck As agents start to act autonomously, finance and operations leaders are quietly panicking about audit trails, approvals and error containment. Recent guidance from the major audit firms has put AI controls squarely on the boardroom agenda, and few internal teams know where to start.
