What's Shifting

Through early 2026, communications buyers have stopped asking agencies whether they use AI and started asking what's left that only humans can do. In-house comms teams are running generative tools for first-draft press releases, media lists, and social copy, and procurement is rewriting retainer scopes around outcomes rather than hours. The mid-tier agency offer — competent execution at a predictable day rate — is the part being quietly hollowed out.

What It Means

For an agency like 7Communications, the pressure is no longer about adopting AI internally; it's about repricing and repositioning before clients do it for you. The opportunity is real, though: the same shift is pushing buyers toward partners who can offer judgement, narrative strategy, and senior counsel that an in-house prompt jockey cannot replicate. The agencies winning right now are the ones drawing a sharper line between commodity output and the thinking that surrounds it.

The Smart Move

The sharpest operators are rebuilding their service menu around two tiers — a low-cost, AI-accelerated production layer and a high-margin advisory layer priced on impact, not effort. They're also being explicit with clients about which tools are in the workflow and where the human judgement sits, because opacity is starting to cost trust. Quietly, several are also using the freed-up capacity to invest in proprietary measurement and audience insight that clients cannot replicate with off-the-shelf AI.

Three Signals to Watch
1
Retainers are being unbundled Recent client RFPs in the UK comms market are splitting "content production" away from "strategic counsel" as separate line items, with very different price expectations for each. Agencies that quote a blended day rate are increasingly being asked to justify it.
2
In-house comms teams are tooling up Enterprise communications platforms have rolled out generative drafting, media monitoring summarisation, and tone-of-voice modelling features in the last few months. Heads of comms now have credible internal alternatives for work that used to flow to agency juniors by default.
3
Disclosure is becoming a buying criterion Regulated-sector clients — financial services, healthcare, public bodies — are starting to require agencies to declare AI use in deliverables and to evidence human review. This is reshaping which agencies make the shortlist before a pitch even begins.
The craft isn't going anywhere — but the proof of it now has to sit in the proposal, not just the press cuttings.
