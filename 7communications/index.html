What's Shifting

Across communications and PR work, generative AI has moved from a curiosity to a quiet co-author inside client teams. Marketing directors at automotive, finance and consumer goods brands are now arriving at agency conversations with AI-drafted positioning, first-pass press releases and synthetic audience research already in hand. The brief is no longer a blank page — it's a draft asking to be made sharper, braver and more human.

What It Means

For a multi-sector communications firm like 7Communications, this changes where value sits in the engagement. The mechanical layers — first drafts, media list building, summary reports — are being absorbed by clients themselves, while judgement, taste, cultural reading and senior counsel become the work clients actually want to pay for. The pressure is real, but so is the opening: agencies that move up the value stack early will set the new price floor for everyone else.

The Smart Move

The sharper operators are rebuilding their offer around editorial judgement and strategic counsel rather than production hours, and they're packaging an internal AI stack that lets a single senior practitioner do work that used to require a pod. They're also training clients to bring AI drafts into the room, then charging confidently for the thinking that turns those drafts into something a CEO would actually say.
